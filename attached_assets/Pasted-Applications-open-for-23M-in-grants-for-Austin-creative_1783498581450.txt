Applications open for $23M in grants for Austin creatives

Austin's Arts, Culture, Music and Entertainment (ACEM) has launched $23 million in grants to support the city's creative community.
Author: Kate Norum
Published: 1:19 PM CDT July 7, 2026
Updated: 4:30 PM CDT July 7, 2026
AUSTIN, Texas — Applications are open for $23 million in grants for Austin’s creative community.
Austin’s Arts, Culture, Music and Entertainment (ACME) department has launched the grants, supported by the city’s hotel occupancy tax revenue, as part of efforts to strengthen the local arts sector.
The funding includes four programs: the Austin Live Music Fund, Elevate, the Heritage Preservation Grant, and Thrive.
City officials said grants ranging from $5,000 to $250,000 will support individual artists, musicians and nonprofit arts organizations. Eligible uses include creative production, public programming, administrative needs, cultural events and digital arts projects.
Funded projects are expected to begin contracting in January 2027, with one- or two-year agreements depending on the program.
Applications close at 6:59 p.m. on Aug. 18, and applications are available here. 
Applicants can get support by attending workshops, joining virtual office hours from 10 a.m. to noon Tuesdays through Aug. 18, scheduling one-on-one meetings with ACME staff, or viewing informational sessions in English and Spanish.
The announcement follows $24 million in grants awarded in March, $3.8 million distributed to Year 2 fiscal 2025 Thrive recipients, and $500,000 awarded in June. Another $500,000 grant cycle is expected to launch in September, bringing the total investment for the year to $51 million.
“Today’s launch reflects how deeply the city values both the creative community and the voices that continue to help shape these programs,” ACME Director Angela Means said in a statement. “These HOT-funded grants are not just awards; they are a reinvestment of visitor dollars into the artists, musicians, cultural organizations and heritage stewards who make Austin a sought-out destination.”
City officials said the latest grants build on its “The Creative Reset” aimed at incorporating community feedback and improving future funding cycles.
“Our team listened closely to community members’ feedback, and the enhancements we have rolled out this year are a direct response,” Means said. “By simplifying access, strengthening support and expanding language and community-based assistance, we are ensuring these funds reach more people, in more places, with greater impact.”
